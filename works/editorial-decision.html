Editorial design process

Design is a decision.
Made once. Felt everywhere.

Most editorial work fills space with decoration. This project removes everything that doesn't serve reading. The result: 3.4× longer engagement.

01 — Work

Measure everything,
keep almost nothing

Started with 47 layout variations. Ended with 3. Every decision was measured against one question: does this help the reader finish the article?

Research
200+

Readers studied with eye-tracking across devices. Found readers skip 73% of decorative elements automatically.

Reduction
68%

Elements removed. Sidebars, pull-quotes, social counters, related posts — all cut if they didn't aid comprehension.

Result
3.4×

Reading time increased. Average went from 1:23 to 4:42. Not by adding features, by removing friction.

02 — Process

Built in a text editor,
not Figma

Designed with real content first. Tested with real readers, not stakeholders.

01

Research — Watch, Don't Ask

We didn't send surveys. We watched 200+ people read. Screen recordings, eye-tracking, scroll maps. Discovered three patterns: F-pattern scanning, automatic ad-blindness, and the 65-character optimal line length.

02

Reduce — Subtract Until It Breaks

Started with 47 elements per page. Removed one at a time, tested with 10 users each time. Stopped when comprehension dropped. Final count: 15 elements. That's a 68% reduction.

03

Refine — Measure Everything

12 rounds of A/B testing. Not on button colors — on reading experience. Line height 1.7 won over 1.5. Font size 18px won over 16px. Margins 32px won over 24px.
